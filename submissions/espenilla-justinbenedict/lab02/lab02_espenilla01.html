In plain words A derivative is a rate: dh/dt says how many metres per hour the level gains, and its maximum tells the instant it rose fastest. The second derivative says whether that rate itself was growing or easing.

What the second derivative says about the inflow

The inflow accelerated into the event and then eased off: the second derivative is strongly positive while the rate builds (max +0.80 m/h² near t = 33.00 h), then crosses to negative and the rate fades (min -0.96 m/h² near t = 46.25 h) — a rain band building and decaying across the catchment, not a gate opened and shut.

Fragility
Differences live only between readings, so they amplify the sensor's rounding: up to 0.04 m/h (first) and 0.16 m/h² (second). That is why Tab 2 fits a curve before trusting instants.

The fit, and the arithmetic behind it

Choose a model, and defend it — before fitting anything.

The choice I fit a sum of two logistics: one rising sigmoid for the filling event and a second, negative one for the drawdown, because a single sigmoid can only rise and settle while this reservoir visibly comes back down. In the comparison below it wins this dataset on every criterion it faces.

Running the fit

Reading R² = 0.999 is flattering, but the residuals are not a clean cloud: they drift, run in same-sign stretches and widen where the level moves fastest. That is a fit that is good but not honest at the centimetre — the two-sigmoid shape cannot follow the slow tail — and I keep that evidence rather than hide it.

Units, and what the number is not

h is in metres, t in hours, so the area is in metre-hours (m·h) — how high, for how long. It is not a volume: a depth log at one point knows nothing about the reservoir's width, so volume would need a stage–area curve this sensor never provides.

The cross-check

quad gives 1260.9708 m·h on the smooth curve; np.trapezoid gives 1260.9963 m·h on the straight chords between readings.

The gap, in one sentence The 0.0254 m·h (0.002%) difference is simply the area the smooth curve adds or loses against straight chords between the rounded readings — the fitted model's belief about what happened between samples versus the trapezoid's assumption of straight lines — and the two agree far better than 0.01%.

What it means to the flood-control office — and what it does not

It is the loading history on the dam wall: the water averaged 17.57 m over the 71.75 h record (area ÷ span). It does not replace the crest (21.14 m, what a warning is issued on) nor the peak rate (0.9600 m/h, what evacuation windows are computed from), and it is not stored volume.
